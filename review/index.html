Step two

Four design directions

Every preview below is live markup rather than a screenshot, so none of them can drift out of step with what it claims to show. One question for each, and it is the only one that matters: would this page make you take the work seriously in the first two seconds?

Direction A

The search page

What ships today. A wordmark, one bar, a microphone and a row of buttons. Everything else — the board, a game, the demo — stays closed until somebody asks for it.

Strongest at: speed, and knowing exactly what to do first. Weakest at: a quiet first screen shows none of the machinery that makes this unusual.

Direction B

Paper and rules

No dark field anywhere. The board becomes a ruled sheet — who did what, in rows. Closer to a document than to a console, and the lightest of the four.

Strongest at: reading as considered and enterprise, and printing well. Weakest at: it looks less like software running and more like a report about software.

Direction C

The terminal

The tool showing its own workings. Monospace throughout, timestamps, exit codes. The page stops describing the system and simply is one.

Strongest at: credibility with a technical reader, and it is the most honest about what this actually is. Weakest at: a non-technical buyer may read it as unfinished.

Direction D

Editorial

The claim leads, the machinery follows. One sentence at full size, the agents reduced to a quiet strip beneath it. Most like a considered consultancy page, least like a console.

Strongest at: a first screen that says what this is in one breath. Weakest at: the agents stop being the demonstration and become a credential.
