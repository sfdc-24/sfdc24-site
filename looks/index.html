Today Looks Back to the board

Four treatments, side by side

The same board, the same words, four ways of presenting them. Each one takes a different position on how much a business page should look like the tool it is describing.

Every preview below is live markup, not a screenshot — so none of them can drift out of step with what it claims to show. Pick one, or pick pieces of several.

Treatment A

The dark field

What ships today. One navy panel carries the chips, the composer and the board, joined by a hairline. The work happens inside a single lit surface on a quiet page.

Strongest at: making the board feel like one instrument. Weakest at: a large dark block on a light page is a commitment, and it dominates the first screen.

Treatment B

Paper and rules

No dark field at all. The board becomes a ruled sheet — who did what, in rows. Closer to a document than to a console, and the lightest of the four.

Strongest at: reading as considered and enterprise, and printing well. Weakest at: it looks less like software running and more like a report about software.

Treatment C

The terminal

The tool showing its own workings. Monospace throughout, timestamps, exit codes. The page stops describing the system and simply is one.

Strongest at: credibility with a technical reader, and it is the most honest about what this actually is. Weakest at: a non-technical buyer may read it as unfinished.

Treatment D

Editorial

The claim leads, the machinery follows. One sentence at full size, the agents reduced to a quiet strip beneath it. Most like a considered consultancy page, least like a console.

Strongest at: a first screen that says what this is in one breath. Weakest at: the agents stop being the demonstration and become a credential.

How to score them

Four questions, because “which do you prefer” produces a preference and not a decision. Score each treatment out of five:

  1. In one glance, is it obvious what this business does? A visitor gives a page a second or two before deciding.
  2. Does it look like the work of a team you would hire? The page is the sample.
  3. Does the board read as work being done, rather than as decoration? That is the whole proposition.
  4. Would it survive being opened on a phone in front of someone? That is where a shared link actually gets read.

Treatments are not exclusive. Taking B’s ruled rows into A’s dark field, or D’s opening line above C’s terminal, are both real options.
